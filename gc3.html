The pod model

A pod is a micro-enterprise, not a department

Each pod carries its own manager, its own specialists and its own agents, and is measured on the work it ships rather than the hours it logs. Pods serve a small client group; dedicated pods are available when the volume justifies it.

Creative Pod — 3 people + 2 agents Digital Pod — 5 people + 4 agents Strategy Pod — 7 people + 6 agents

Why the model works

  • Built for speed — no bureaucratic handoffs between four vendors.
  • Quality comes from shared design systems, guild standards and process frameworks, not from heroics.
  • Cost is predictable — a subscription and a credit pool, not an estimate that becomes an argument.
  • Every sprint trains the automation layer, so month six costs less to run than month one.

Human-in-the-loop

Half the work is done by agents. Half by a named specialist.

A pod is not a team of people using tools, and it is not an agent running unattended. Agents take the brief, draft the documentation and produce the first pass; named specialists review, correct and finish it. Nothing ships that a human has not signed off.

What we are not

Plainly, so nobody is surprised later

Not a BPO

We do not sell seats on a process you have already designed. We design the work and then run it.

Not a dev shop

Engineering is one surface of one product. It is not the business, and it is not sold as a body shop.

Not a call center

There is no volume metric on this engagement. Output is judged on whether it moved something.

Not unlimited

Credits are finite and visible. A pod that runs out of credits tells you, rather than silently absorbing the overrun.

Not fully automated

Agents draft and repeat. They do not decide. Every deliverable has a human name attached to it.

Not yet complete

Several products in our stack are in active development. We label them as such on the product pages rather than implying they are finished.
